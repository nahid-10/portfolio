LOAD TEST · 6-STAGE RAMP
LightBaseline traffic — zero failures
ModerateRoughly 7× baseline traffic — still zero failures
HeavierFirst failures appear
HighFailure rate creeps up on several features at once
OverloadFailures rise sharply; rate-limiting kicks in
Breaking pointMajority of calls fail or get rate-limited

Bottom line: the ramp mapped a clear concurrency threshold where failure rates start climbing, and a sharper cliff further out where the service can no longer keep up — giving the team a real, evidence-based capacity boundary instead of a guess.

How I decide what to automate How I actually think through a test plan before writing one.
  1. Map flows by business risk, not ease of testing. Checkout and auth outrank low-traffic admin screens, regardless of which is simpler to script.
  2. Automate what repeats and wire it into CI; keep exploration where judgment matters. Stable, high-frequency paths get Playwright/Postman suites running on every push. New or UX-sensitive features stay manual until they stabilize.
  3. Triage by blast radius. Who hits this, and how often, usually matters more than whatever severity got typed into the ticket — a cosmetic bug on the payment page can outrank a "major" bug on a screen nobody visits.
